# Translational Work

## Why We Do This

NCIL's mission is to conduct research that ultimately helps people live healthier, happier, and more productive lives. Basic research is the first step in that, but a finding in a journal article does not by itself become something anyone can use. Getting there takes a different kind of work — engineering, regulatory approval, manufacturing, support — and often a different kind of organization.

## Zeuron Inc.

Lab director Aaron Newman, with former lab member Hamid Mandi, co-founded [Zeuron Inc.](https://zeuron.io) to carry the lab's brain-computer interface research into products that people can actually use.

Zeuron's current focus is **GamBit**, an assistive technology that aims to enrich the social lives of people with physical mobility limitations — such as spinal cord injury, cerebral palsy, and ALS — by enabling them to play video games using brain activity. Video games are, for many people, a primary way of spending time with friends and family. For someone who cannot use a conventional controller, that avenue is largely closed. GamBit is an attempt to reopen it.

The underlying science comes directly from the lab's work on [brain-computer interfaces](BCI): the problem of reliably detecting which item a user is attending to, for any user, at any time.

## Current State of Project

This work is ongoing. When recruitment for participants is active, it is advertised on SONA, Dalhousie University's internal system for participating in studies in return for course credit. If you are interested in participating, please look for the study on [SONA](https://dalpsyc.sona-systems.com/default.aspx). 

## For Students

There are opportunities for students to get involved in data collection and analysis on NCIL research studies that will inform the development of GamBit. We also seek students with experience in game development to help integrate BCI stimuli into popular game designs.
