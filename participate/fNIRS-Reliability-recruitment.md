# Reliability of fNIRS Signals
{:.center}

We are recruiting Dalhousie students through SONA. Please continue reading for more information!

## What Is the Purpose of the Project?
Functional near-infrared spectroscopy (fNIRS) is a safe, non-invasive brain imaging technique that measures changes in blood oxygenation in the brain. Before we can use it to study how children learn to read, we need to know how consistent its measurements are from one day to the next. That is what this study tests. Read more about the [fNIRS reliability project here](https://ncil.science/projects/fNIRS-Reliability).

## What Does a Participant Do?
Participants come to the NCIL lab, located in the Life Sciences Centre on Dalhousie University's Studley campus, on **two occasions one week apart**. At each visit you will perform the same reading task while we record fNIRS. fNIRS involves wearing a soft cap on the head that holds light sensors against the scalp — nothing is injected, and no radiation is involved.
